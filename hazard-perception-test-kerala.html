01 · The basics

What is a Hazard Perception Test?

A Hazard Perception Test (HPT) checks whether a driver can notice risks developing around a vehicle and recognise the right moment to respond. It is about seeing beyond what is directly in front of the vehicle: observing people, vehicles, road conditions and clues that may signal danger a few seconds ahead.

During a video-based hazard perception assessment, a learner watches a recorded driving scene from the driver's point of view. The scene may show traffic moving normally at first. As it develops, a pedestrian may approach the edge of the road, a vehicle may begin to pull out, or an obstruction may appear around a bend. The learner must recognise the developing hazard and respond at the appropriate moment using the input method specified for that assessment.

Good hazard perception is not simply reacting after something dangerous has happened. It means noticing early clues, anticipating what could happen next and choosing a safe response before the situation becomes an emergency.

👀

See more than the road ahead

Scan the road, footpaths, junctions, mirrors and road edges. Look for movement and behaviour that may change the traffic situation.

🧠

Think ahead

Ask what another road user might do next. A person standing near a kerb or a vehicle waiting at a side road may become a hazard.

🛑

Respond safely

Adjust speed, increase following distance and prepare to brake or stop when the situation requires it.

02 · Recognising danger

What are hazards in driving?

A hazard is anything on or near the road that could make a driver slow down, change direction or take another action to avoid danger. A developing hazard is a situation that is changing and may soon require the driver to act.

🚶

Pedestrians and children

A person approaching the kerb, children near a school gate or someone stepping out from between parked vehicles.

🏍️

Motorcycles and bicycles

A two-wheeler moving into a blind spot, changing lanes suddenly or approaching between slow-moving vehicles.

🚗

Vehicles entering or changing direction

A car reversing, a bus pulling away from a stop, a vehicle emerging from a side road or an unsafe overtake.

🐕

Animals on the road

A dog, cattle or another animal near the carriageway that may move into the vehicle's path without warning.

🌧️

Weather and visibility

Heavy rain, waterlogging, glare, fog, poor night visibility or a wet surface that increases stopping distance.

🕳️

Road surface and obstacles

Potholes, loose gravel, fallen branches, construction materials, oil, debris or a damaged road edge.

↪️

Bends, junctions and limited views

A blind curve, hidden driveway, narrow bridge or junction where another road user may appear suddenly.

🚌

Stopped buses and parked vehicles

Passengers may cross from in front of a bus; a door may open; pedestrians or vehicles may be hidden from view.

These are general road-safety examples for learning. The exact clips and hazards used in an official examination may differ.

03 · Spot it early

How to identify a developing hazard

The key is to observe clues—not wait until the danger is directly in the vehicle's path.

👣

Watch people's movement

Notice pedestrians looking towards the road, approaching a crossing, moving around a parked vehicle or standing close to the kerb.

💡

Read vehicle clues

Look for brake lights, indicator signals, front wheels turning, reversing lights or a vehicle slowly edging out of a junction.

🌧️

Read the road and weather

Look further ahead for standing water, slippery patches, loose material, poor visibility and changes in road width or surface.

Use a simple observation routine

  1. Look well ahead and scan the road edges.
  2. Check mirrors and blind spots before changing position.
  3. Identify people or vehicles whose movement may affect your path.
  4. Predict what could happen next.
  5. Choose a safe response early—slow down, create space or stop when needed.

What should a learner avoid?

  • Looking only at the vehicle immediately in front.
  • Following another vehicle too closely.
  • Assuming a pedestrian or driver will wait.
  • Braking or swerving suddenly without checking surrounding traffic.
  • Using a phone or allowing distractions while driving.
04 · Kerala examination

Kerala Hazard Perception Test: announced exam format

Kerala has announced a computer-based Hazard Perception Test as an additional stage in the driving-licence testing process. The format below is based on reporting about the Transport Commissioner's September 2026 announcement; candidates should check the latest official department directions before their test.

🎬

10 video clips

The announced format describes ten driving-scene video clips, each approximately one minute long.

⚠️

Hazards in each clip

Reports describe up to two potential hazards in a clip. Candidates identify the developing hazards while the scene plays.

🖱️

Click at the right moment

The reported method uses a computer input such as a mouse or tracker to mark when a developing hazard is recognised.

💯

100 total marks

The ten clips are reported to carry ten marks each, for a combined total of one hundred marks.

⏱️

Timing matters

A timely response can receive full marks. Delayed responses may receive fewer marks, according to the announced format.

🎯

60 marks to pass

The reported minimum qualifying score is sixty out of one hundred. Confirm the current official pass criteria before attending.

Where does HPT fit into the driving-licence process?

Learner's Licence test

First, the applicant completes the online learner's licence knowledge test and meets the applicable requirements.

Waiting period

The announced process describes a thirty-day interval after passing the learner's test before the HPT stage.

Computer-based HPT

The applicant completes the video-based hazard perception assessment and must meet the qualifying score.

Ground and road tests

Applicants who qualify proceed to the practical stages, subject to the department's current process.

05 · For driving schools

What should driving schools in Kerala do to prepare?

Hazard perception training adds a new teaching topic to the work of a motor driving school. Instructors can help learners build observation and anticipation skills before the examination—not simply teach them to click quickly during a video.

Practical preparation checklist

  • Explain the difference between a static object, a potential hazard and a developing hazard.
  • Use Kerala-relevant examples: school zones, bus stops, narrow roads, junctions, rain, two-wheelers, pedestrians and animals.
  • Provide regular computer-based video practice and explain why a situation is becoming risky.
  • Review missed or late responses with learners and discuss safer choices.
  • Keep computers, displays, mouse or other required input devices and a reliable internet connection ready where online practice is used.
  • Check official MVD circulars and local RTO instructions for the current examination arrangements.
🏫

How a school-based HPT practice platform can help

A structured digital practice system can help a driving school organise student access, give learners repeated video-based exercises and review their performance. It can supplement instructor-led road-safety education; it cannot replace practical driving lessons or official examination arrangements.

06 · Learner preparation

How learners can prepare for the HPT

Practise observation

Watch road scenes and identify more than one possible risk. Include pavements, junctions, side roads, parked vehicles and road edges in your scan.

Understand why it is risky

Do not memorise a click point alone. Explain what changed in the scene and why it could affect the driver's path or stopping distance.

Build consistent habits

Practise in short, regular sessions. Discuss mistakes with an instructor and apply the same observation habits during supervised road driving.

The purpose of practice is to improve real road awareness. A learner should never try to use a phone, click a device or interact with a screen while driving a real vehicle.

07 · Common questions

Hazard Perception Test Kerala – FAQs

What is the difference between a hazard and a developing hazard?

A hazard is something that could create danger. A developing hazard is a situation changing in a way that may soon require the driver to slow down, stop or change position. For example, a parked car is a possible obstruction; a person stepping out from behind it is a developing hazard.

When is Kerala's new Hazard Perception Test expected to start?

The rollout has been reported as starting on 1 December 2026. Applicants and driving schools should confirm the latest date and instructions through official Kerala MVD or RTO communications.

How many videos are in the announced HPT exam?

The announced format reported in September 2026 describes ten clips of approximately one minute each, with a total of 100 marks.

What is the pass mark for the Kerala HPT?

Reports about the announced format state a qualifying score of 60 out of 100. Check the latest official instructions for confirmation.

Is the Hazard Perception Test the same as the Learner's Licence test?

No. The Learner's Licence test checks knowledge of road rules and signs. The HPT is a separate video-based assessment of hazard recognition and timely response.

Does this website conduct the official MVD test?

No. This website is an independent learning and practice platform. It is not operated by or affiliated with the Kerala Motor Vehicles Department and does not issue licences, official HPT results or government certificates.

Can a driving school in Kerala enquire about access?

Yes. A driving school can contact the HPT team on WhatsApp at 8943764734 to enquire about registration and school access.
