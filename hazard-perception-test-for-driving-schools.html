Understand the skill

What is a Hazard Perception Test?

Safe driving is not only about controlling a vehicle. It is also about noticing what may happen next — before a situation turns into an emergency.

See the developing hazard — not just the obvious danger

A hazard is something on or near the road that may require a driver to slow down, stop, change direction or take another safe action. A developing hazard is a situation that is starting to create a risk, such as a pedestrian moving towards the road or a vehicle beginning to pull out.

Hazard perception focuses on observation, anticipation, judgement and timely response. The learner must look ahead, notice changes around the vehicle and decide when a situation is becoming risky.

A simple learner flow

How does the video-based test work?

The learner watches a driving scene and responds when a hazard begins to develop. Faster and correctly timed responses earn stronger scores.

01→

Watch the road scene

A short, realistic driving clip plays on the screen. The learner observes the road, traffic and surroundings.

02→

Spot the developing hazard

The learner watches for changes that may create danger — not just obstacles that are already obvious.

03→

Tap or click promptly

When the hazard starts to develop, the learner taps the video on a touchscreen or clicks with a mouse.

04✓

Receive the result

The platform evaluates the responses, records the score and displays the learner’s performance summary.

Kerala’s HPT development

Kerala’s Motor Vehicles Department has announced the inclusion of a computer-based Hazard Perception Test in its driving-licence process from 1 December 2026. News reports describe a state-specific format of ten one-minute clips, with a maximum of two hazards per clip and a 60-mark pass threshold.

This is a Kerala-specific government development, not a statement that every Indian state uses the same statutory test. Read the Onmanorama report and The New Indian Express report, and verify the latest circulars directly with the relevant MVD/RTO.

Independent platform notice

Hazard Perception Test is an independent digital school-level training and assessment platform. It is not a government website, RTO portal or official licensing examination service. It does not issue driving licences or replace any mandatory assessment prescribed by a state or central transport authority. Driving schools should follow the official instructions applicable in their jurisdiction.

Answers for school owners

Frequently asked questions

Useful information to help you decide how to introduce hazard-perception learning at your school.

Is this platform suitable for driving schools outside Kerala?

Yes. School registration supports states and Union Territories across India. Outside Kerala, the platform should be treated as a supplementary school-level hazard-awareness assessment, subject to local rules and requirements.

Is this the official government HPT examination?

No. It is an independent digital learning and assessment platform. It is not operated by the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways, a state MVD or an RTO, and it does not issue official examination results or licences.

How many clips are included in the platform test?

The current platform assessment selects ten active video clips in a randomised order. Each clip has a 50-second time limit.

How does a learner respond to a hazard?

The learner taps the video on a touchscreen or clicks the video with a mouse when they notice a developing hazard. Correct, timely responses receive stronger marks under the platform scoring rules.

Can we use our own driving school logo?

Yes. During school registration, a school can provide its logo. The school name and logo are used in the branded school experience. If a school has no logo, the platform’s default identity can be used.

What does the school administrator see?

The school portal includes a school leaderboard and performance information. The provider-side administration area has broader school and candidate management tools.

What equipment is required?

A modern web browser and a reliable internet connection are required. The platform is responsive and can be opened on a desktop, laptop, tablet or compatible smartphone. A larger screen is generally more comfortable for video assessment.

How do we enquire or register?
